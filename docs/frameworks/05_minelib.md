# 05, the MineLib lane

> 21 KB of shipped source (`frontend/src/opt/realCases.ts`, `frontend/src/opt/minelib.ts`) that carried the
> product's headline claim and was documented nowhere until 2026-08-21.

The MineLib lane is what lets PitForge say its optimiser is **exact** without asking anyone to take that on
faith. It solves published benchmark instances whose optimal values were computed and published by other
people, and puts its answer beside theirs.

## What MineLib is

MineLib (Espinoza, Goycoolea, Moreno and Newman, *Annals of Operations Research*, 2013,
doi:10.1007/s10479-012-1258-3) is a public library of open-pit mine-planning instances with published optimal
values for three problem types. PitForge uses the **UPIT** (ultimate pit) values, and, in the scheduling lane,
one published **CPIT** scenario (see [04, scheduling](04_scheduling.md)).

## The licence posture, stated precisely

MineLib is published under **CC BY-SA 3.0 Unported**. That licence would permit redistribution with
attribution. PitForge still keeps the instance bytes **out of git**, and that is a project data-minimisation
choice, not a licence restriction. Only attributed summary numbers are committed. Saying this accurately
matters: earlier revisions of this repository described the instances as downloadable only under a
restricted academic arrangement, which is not a thing that exists for MineLib. The reference-integrity gate
bans that phrasing by exact substring, which is also why this paragraph paraphrases it instead of quoting
it: the gate cannot tell an assertion from a refutation, and a dumb matcher with no false negatives is worth
more here than a clever one.

Practically:

- published instances are fetched at runtime into `frontend/.minelib-cache/` (**gitignored**);
- `frontend/scripts/fetch-minelib.mjs` does the fetching, from public mirrors;
- the synthetic twins ARE committed, under `frontend/public/twins/`, because they are ours (MIT, generated by
  [`oreblocks`](https://pypi.org/project/oreblocks/) 0.1.0).

## What the lane ships

Six cases, in two families. The relative error is against each case's published or stamped optimum.

| id | family | published optimum | rel. error |
|---|---|---|---|
| `newman1` | published MineLib | 26,086,899 | 9.96e-10 |
| `zuck_small` | published MineLib | 1,422,726,898 | 1.86e-10 |
| `kd` | published MineLib | 652,195,037 | 1.30e-10 |
| `twin-porphyry-s` | synthetic twin (ours) | 126,908,453.74 | 1.88e-12 |
| `twin-vein-m` | synthetic twin (ours) | 213,735,284.68 | 8.35e-11 |
| `twin-corehalo-m` | synthetic twin (ours) | 65,696,305.79 | 9.25e-11 |

All six match. The public surfaces claim `<= 2e-9`, and `frontend/test/minelib.test.ts` now asserts exactly
that rather than the looser `1e-6` it used to.

**Solve times are deliberately not in that table.** They are machine-dependent: the same instance measured
111, 234 and 527 ms across three runs on one laptop, so a decimal figure is not a property of the product.
`data/derived/minelib-results.json` (schema `pitforge.minelib-bench/v3`) records timings together with the
`timingEnvironment` they were measured in, and the external surfaces quote scale rather than decimals.

## The eleven instances, and the eight not solved here

MineLib publishes **eleven** UPIT instances. Three are solved above; the other eight are listed in the
artifact's `excluded` array, each with a reason, so the artifact never implies the library is smaller than it
is:

- `marvin`: ships with commercial Whittle software; no verified public mirror.
- `mclaughlin_limit` and `mclaughlin`: no verified public mirror; `mclaughlin` is 2.14 M blocks and roughly 73 M
  arcs, untested at that scale for this Dinic engine.
- `zuck_medium`, `p4hd`, `w23`, `zuck_large` and `sm2`: not attempted, no verified public mirror located by this
  project. No optimum is asserted for an instance we have not solved.

Undercounting the exclusions loses credit for no reason, which is why the count is gated (see below).

## The size gate

`newman1` solves **live in the page**. `zuck_small` and `kd` sit behind an explicit size gate, because a
14,153-block instance with 219,778 precedences is not something to start on a phone without asking. The gate
is a product decision, not a limitation of the engine: the same engine solves them in the offline bake.

## Scenario knobs are LOCKED in real mode

The published instances carry their own per-block net values and their own explicit precedence lists. If the
app re-derived values from price and cost sliders, the answer would no longer be comparable to the published
optimum, and the whole point of the lane would be lost. So in real mode the knobs are locked and the UI says
why.

## Where it is verified

| what | where |
|---|---|
| the optima reproduce, `<= 2e-9` | `frontend/test/minelib.test.ts` (skips without the cache) |
| the tests actually RUN | `.github/workflows/minelib-oracle.yml`, which fetches the instances and **fails if any oracle test reports skipped**, on a schedule and on every release tag |
| the artifact is present and internally consistent | `scripts/check_headline_artifacts.py`, in CI and before every deploy |
| the bake ships everything it declares | `frontend/scripts/bake-minelib.mjs` raises rather than writing a partial artifact |

That second row exists because it was the gap: the oracle tests are gated on a gitignored cache, no workflow
ever fetched it, and a skipped test is a green test, so the headline claim was verified nowhere in CI.

## Source map

| file | what it holds |
|---|---|
| `frontend/src/opt/realCases.ts` | the six case definitions: ids, block/precedence counts, published optima, gate level, provenance in EN and ES, and the URLs each case's data comes from |

| `frontend/src/viz/RealCasePanel.tsx` | the in-app panel, including the licence caption and the live solve time |
